# Implementation plan: federated hot leg over pgx (#719)

This plan builds the design in [design.md §6.1.1](design.md). The federated pass stops reading PostgreSQL through DuckDB. It reads the dirty set and the hot keys over pgx in one `REPEATABLE READ READ ONLY` snapshot, and DuckDB reads only the parquet tiers. Go merges the two results. Once this lands and #785 is in place, no Forma deployment needs `postgres_scanner`. #631 is then superseded, and #691 and #754 have nothing left to assess.

## Before the first change

Three inputs gate the work. The first two gate the switch of the default path, not the code.

- **The #728 ruling.** Under this design PostgreSQL filters the hot rows and DuckDB filters the parquet rows of one answer. Until each difference has a ruling and the ruling ships on both dialects, a row's inclusion depends on whether it has been flushed. The tier parity suite (step 6) fails on such a difference until then. #763 is ruled and needs no change on either dialect: a non-canonical rfc3339 image is outside the query contract, so the suite's fixtures hold canonical images only (`docs/rfc3339-storage-contract.md`).
- **The latency baseline** that #719 asked for. It measures how federated latency splits between the PostgreSQL copy and the parquet scan on today's path, with the benchmark harness, at several `entity_main` sizes for a fixed hot set. Steps 3 and 5 compare against it. Without it, "cheaper" in the design is a claim nobody has checked.

  *Measured (#851).* `TestHotLegLatencyBaseline` in the production harness loads `e2e_wide` rows (nine `eav_data` rows each), flushes them, consolidates the parquet into base files, and vacuums and analyzes the three tables. It then writes a hot set of 1,000 rows through the EntityManager, 500 updates of flushed rows and 500 creates, so the dirty set holds 1,000 row IDs and 500 parquet versions it must discard. Two query shapes run on the `attach` path: a `title` equality that selects one row, and an unfiltered first page sorted by `count`.

  ```
  FORMA_HOT_LEG_BASELINE_SIZES=10000,100000,300000,1000000 \
    go test ./internal/e2e_harness/production -tags=e2e -run TestHotLegLatencyBaseline -v -timeout 3h
  ```

  Each shape is measured in two phases. The engine pass comes first: the engine's own query, timed while the DuckDB instance holds nothing but the catalog the engine attaches. That is the latency steps 3 and 5 compare against. Then the engine's rendered statement is replayed on the same instance and the same attached catalog, alternately as rendered (full) and retargeted at a hot-only control: a schema in the same PostgreSQL database holding only the `change_log` rows the statement's hot-leg predicate keeps, and the `entity_main` and `eav_data` rows of those row IDs, with the full tables' `pg_class` statistics. The control keeps the dirty set, the hot rows, the answer, the parquet objects, the catalog and its settings, including filter pushdown off. All it drops is the flushed rows that the statement copies from PostgreSQL and then discards. A profiled run of each confirms that DuckDB plans the two identically, operator for operator (45 operators, the same join order and the same projections at every size). The two replays then differ only in how many rows the PostgreSQL scans return: 12,000 for the control against 12 million for the full tables at 1,000,000 rows.

  Full minus hot-only is therefore a differential estimate, not a timed stage. It is what copying the flushed rows and discarding them costs the pass: their transfer from PostgreSQL, plus DuckDB's pivot, joins and filter over them. The pgx hot leg removes exactly this. The two parts cannot be told apart this way, and DuckDB's profiler cannot split them either: the postgres extension's scans report no operator time, whatever they return. The hot-only replay is the parquet scan and merge plus the copy of the 12,000 hot rows, so it is an upper bound on the parquet side.

  i7-8700K, 8 threads, 15 GB; PostgreSQL 16 and RustFS in local Docker containers; DuckDB 2.0 at the factory defaults (4 threads, 4,096 MB). Median of five runs, in ms, after one warm-up:

  | `entity_main` rows | Shape | Engine pass | Full replay | Hot-only replay | Flushed rows' cost (full − hot-only) | Share of the full replay |
  | ---: | :---- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
  | 10,000 | one row by `title` | 123 | 99 | 38 | 61 | 62% |
  | 10,000 | first page by `count` | 149 | 134 | 68 | 66 | 49% |
  | 100,000 | one row by `title` | 400 | 384 | 50 | 333 | 87% |
  | 100,000 | first page by `count` | 650 | 659 | 254 | 405 | 61% |
  | 300,000 | one row by `title` | 977 | 976 | 61 | 915 | 94% |
  | 300,000 | first page by `count` | 1,763 | 1,869 | 723 | 1,146 | 61% |
  | 1,000,000 | one row by `title` | 3,111 | 3,328 | 94 | 3,234 | 97% |
  | 1,000,000 | first page by `count` | 5,937 | 5,926 | 2,399 | 3,527 | 60% |

  The flushed rows' cost grows linearly with the tables, 3.0 to 3.3 ms per 1,000 `entity_main` rows for the selective shape, while the hot set stays fixed. At 1,000,000 rows it is 3.2 to 3.5 seconds of every pass. The rest of the pass stays under 100 ms for the selective shape and reaches 2.4 seconds only where the page ranks every parquet row. The design's premise holds on this data: copying the flushed rows dominates the selective pass at every size from 100,000 rows up, and it scales with the table, not with the hot set. Where the page ranks every row, it is still 60% of the pass. The pgx hot leg replaces it with its own snapshot reads, which this baseline does not measure.

  Three things limit the numbers. First, the rows are clones of 500 templates written through the EntityManager, which writes about 34 rows a second on the harness. Only `title` and `count` vary per clone, so the parquet compresses better than real data would. Second, at 10,000 rows the engine pass runs 15 to 25 ms above the full replay. From 100,000 rows up the two agree within 7%. The fixed gap at the smallest size has not been investigated. Third, the tables are vacuumed before the timings. In an earlier run without that step, the selective shape's engine pass came out 24 to 49% above its replay at 100,000 and 300,000 rows. That run timed the engine first after the bulk load, likely while autovacuum was still working through it.

## Steps

Each step is one PR and leaves `main` releasable. Steps 1 to 5 change no answer, with two exceptions, both in step 2 and both on today's path too. The cursor normalization refuses a hand-built cursor whose boundary does not match its column's type. A cursor that names `deleted_ts` reads the column's NULL as `0`: on the rows that tie the cursor before that column, it admits the hot rows and live pre-#274 rows it drops today wherever it admits the rows holding `0` (ascending below or at `0`, descending at or above `0`), and it orders them by the later cursor columns instead of last (design.md §6.1.1 tabulates the boundaries). The new path stays off until step 6.

1. **The snapshot reader and the `change_log` index.** Replace `PostgresDirtyIDFetcher`'s strict fetch with a reader that, in one read-only `REPEATABLE READ` transaction, returns the dirty set for either tier form. The hot form reads the widened `flushed_at = 0 OR flushed_at >= cutoff`, the hot-excluded form the strict set. The reader also returns `HasParquetHistory` from the same snapshot. Add the `(schema_id, flushed_at)` index, including `row_id` and `changed_at`, to `init-db`. Measure staging the dirty set through the Appender against binding it as a `UUID[]` parameter, at 1,000, 10,000 and 100,000 ids. The result decides the transport in step 3. Validation: `EXPLAIN` in an integration test shows the dirty-set read using the index, and the result matches today's sets.

   *Measured (#849).* `BenchmarkDirtySetStaging` stages the set and runs the template's anti-join over a 200,000-row stand-in for the parquet scan, half of the dirty IDs with a scanned copy, on one DuckDB connection (i7-8700K, 8 threads, median of three):

   | Dirty IDs | Appender into a temp table | One `UUID[]` parameter |
   | :---- | :---- | :---- |
   | 1,000 | 2.6 ms | 3.1 ms |
   | 10,000 | 4.2 ms | 15.2 ms |
   | 100,000 | 14.1 ms | 127.9 ms |

   The parameter's cost grows close to linearly with the set past 10,000 IDs and reaches nine times the Appender's at 100,000, and duckdb-go binds only a `[]duckdb.UUID` as a `UUID[]`, so every ID is also converted in Go. Step 3 stages through the Appender. Neither transport changes the statement text with the set's size, so the choice does not touch the plan cache. The numbers are from an in-memory table, not S3 parquet: they compare the transports, not the cost of a pass. The engine still reads the dirty set through `FetchDirtyRowIDs` after this step; the snapshot reader has no caller until step 5.

2. **Sort keys and the comparator, in Go.** Derive each hot sort key from its stored value by the CDC export's rules (design.md §6.1.1, the table under *Ordering parity*). Reuse #785's stage decode where it applies. Add the comparator, with `NULLS LAST` in both directions, and the three-valued keyset predicate. Both take the page's order as input: the offset form's `ORDER BY` with its `row_id ASC`, or the keyset form's cursor columns with each column's direction, `row_id` included (design.md §6.1.1, *The page's order*). Add the cursor normalization (design.md §6.1.1, *One boundary type per cursor column*) to `validateKeysetCursor`, where both transports pass, and render each keyset parameter as `CAST(? AS <export type>)`. Validation: a lockstep test writes rows that reach each derivation's edges, flushes them, and requires the parquet leg's values and `ORDER BY` order to match the Go derivation and comparator. Before writing the `DOUBLE` comparator, a test against DuckDB checks how the engine orders NaN and -0. A cursor parity test binds boundaries of every Go type a caller can pass against every export type: a `float64` 9.6 and whole floats on a `BIGINT` column, integers at 2^53 - 1, 2^53 and 2^53 + 1 and at the `BIGINT` limits on `BIGINT` and `DOUBLE` columns, a NaN boundary, and upper-case uuid text. Each boundary is either refused before either leg runs, or the rendered DuckDB predicate and the Go predicate admit the same rows. A tiebreak test does the same for both `row_id` directions: over rows that tie on every column before `row_id`, the Go comparator orders them as `buildKeysetOrderBy` orders them under `row_id ASC` and `row_id DESC`, and as `buildNonKeysetOrderBy` orders them, and the Go predicate at a `row_id` boundary inside the run admits the rows DuckDB admits.

   The same step applies the `deleted_ts` rule (design.md §6.1.1, *`deleted_ts` reads `0`*). The hot key derives as `0`. The shared keyset renderer references the column as `COALESCE(deleted_ts, 0)` in the predicate and the `ORDER BY`, and leaves the dedup window and the outer select alone. Validation, over one fixture whose live rows are in every encoding (hot, a parquet row exported since #274, and a pre-#274 delta row with a NULL `deleted_at`), on today's path now and on both transports from step 5:
   - *Keyset form.* Walk the fixture with cursors on `deleted_ts, row_id`, ascending and descending, at the boundary `0`, one row per page, and flush the hot rows between two pages. The walk returns every row exactly once, in `row_id` order. Before this step, both walks drop every hot row and every pre-#274 row.
   - *Boundaries.* One page per direction at the boundaries -1, 0 and 1, with `deleted_ts` leading, on the `attach` path, pins design.md's table row by row: ascending at -1 and descending at 1 return every row, the cursors at `0` the rows after their `row_id`, ascending at 1 and descending at -1 none. Each case asserts the NULL-encoded rows by name, since before this step the cases that admit rows return only the rows holding `0`. A cursor on `created_at, deleted_ts, row_id` over rows that share a `created_at` across the encodings pins the order change: the rows come back in `row_id` order, where today the NULL rows come last. The rendered predicate and the Go predicate admit the same rows at each of these boundaries.
   - *Offset form.* A request that sorts on `deleted_ts` is refused before either leg runs, so offset pages cannot reach the column. An offset walk over the same fixture, with the same flush between two pages, returns every row exactly once.

   **Decision point:** sort keys on list attributes. Either implement DuckDB's `LIST` order, or refuse such a sort on the federated route with a 4xx. *Decided (#853):* refused. A federated request that sorts on a list attribute fails as invalid input before routing, so the answer does not depend on the route. Implementing the `LIST` order later lifts the refusal.

   *Built (#853).* `internal/sortkey` holds the export types, the derivation, the comparator, the predicate and the normalization; nothing calls the derivation or the comparator until steps 4 and 5. The tests:
   - *Lockstep.* `TestSortKeyDerivationLockstep` writes `e2e_wide` rows at the edges the write funnel admits and flushes them. Some edges it cannot reach: the write funnel refuses NaN, ±Infinity and images past their declared width, and `e2e_wide` binds no rfc3339, `bool_smallint` or `bool_text` column. `TestDeriveLockstepWithExport` covers those by running the export's own casts over each staged value in DuckDB. It also shows DuckDB's `TRY_CAST` from `DOUBLE` to an integer type rounding half to even.
   - *DuckDB's `DOUBLE` order.* `TestDuckDBDoubleOrder` pins it.
   - *Cursor and `row_id` parity.* `TestKeysetCursorParity` and `TestKeysetRowIDTiebreakParity` run the renderer's SQL over an in-memory table that stands in for the visible CTE.
   - *`deleted_ts`.* `TestDeletedTSKeysetWalk` and `TestDeletedTSKeysetBoundaries` cover the walks, boundaries and offset cases. Their pre-#274 rows are a flushed delta object rewritten in place with a NULL `deleted_at`.

   Without the `COALESCE`, the walks did not drop every NULL row as predicted above. The descending walk returned only the three exported rows. The ascending walk also returned the three hot rows, because the flush between its pages rewrote them as `0` before the cursor reached them. Two of the repository's e2e tests had hand-built `float64` boundaries for the `INTEGER` column `count`. The normalization refuses them, and they now pass integers.

4. **The hot leg's PostgreSQL queries.** Add the hot-keys query: the PostgreSQL route's condition (`ToDualClauses`' PostgreSQL payload), restricted to `entity_main` rows with an entry in the dirty window, `ver_ts` from the newest entry. Add the page read by `row_id`, reusing the PostgreSQL route's reader. Validation: the condition-DSL parity cases run against the hot-keys query and the PostgreSQL route, and must select the same rows.

   *Built (#855).* `DBPersistentRecordRepository.QueryHotKeys` and `ReadHotRows` take the pass's transaction, so step 5 reads them in the snapshot it opens for the dirty set. Nothing calls them yet.
   - *The condition.* It renders through the PostgreSQL route's own builder (`buildHybridConditions`), anchored on `entity_main`, not through `ToDualClauses`. Its PostgreSQL clause (`PgClause`) tests every attribute against `eav_data`, where a column-bound attribute has no row, and its main-table fragment is the `?`-placeholder text `pg_source` embeds in DuckDB. The route itself anchors on `entity_main` whenever a row without any `eav_data` row could match (`is_null`, no condition, #646), and the hot keys are `entity_main` rows. Text ranges therefore render `COLLATE "C"` as the route does, and keep #728's byte order whatever the database's collation.
   - *The window.* It is read as the dirty set's two ranges of the `(schema_id, flushed_at)` index. `ver_ts` is the newest `changed_at` among the row's entries in the window, as `pg_source` ranks them today.
   - *The keys.* Each sort column reads the stored value its key derives from: the bound column, or every `eav_data` value of an EAV-only attribute. Go reduces those to the greatest derived value, as the export's `MAX(CASE WHEN attr_id = … END)` collapses them. `TestDuckDBMaxMatchesDeriveEAVAttribute` checks that reduction against DuckDB's `MAX`, NaN included. `deleted_ts` reads nothing and derives `0`.
   - *The page read.* It refuses a row ID its snapshot does not hold rather than answer a short page: the keys came from the same snapshot, so a missing row means a key from another one.

   `TestHotKeysQueryConditionDSLParity` (production harness) runs the condition-DSL parity cases of `TestConditionDSLOperatorParityAcrossTiers` against the hot-keys query and the PostgreSQL route. With every row hot, the hot keys are exactly the route's rows. Over that test's cold, warm and hot layout, at the widest cutoff and at the strict one, they are the route's rows inside the window. Text ranges over `"a"`, `"B"`, `"b"`, `"Z"` and `"é"` select by bytes on both. In package `internal` the real-PostgreSQL condition-DSL truth table and the `starts_with`/`contains` operand cases also run through both. Further tests there pin the window's bounds, the derivation for each storage form and both `value_numeric` types, and the page read against `Get` inside a snapshot that a later commit does not change.

5. **Engine wiring, behind a switch.** Add `DuckDBConfig.HotLegTransport` (`attach`, the default, or `pgx`). The `pgx` pass runs the six steps of design.md §6.1.1: snapshot, sort, stage, DuckDB, merge, page read. The same step also changes the following:
   - totals and the #181 recount;
   - the keyset coordinator's cursor-free recount;
   - the frozen inventory (design.md §6.1.1): each glob of a source-authored path set is listed at resolution, and the scan reads the listed objects. A failed listing fails the pass as a degradable `ErrFederatedReadFailed`. A listed object deleted before the scan fails the read; its classification must not claim a manifest inconsistency (`ErrParquetSetInconsistent`), since no manifest listed it;
   - the #720 empty-set short-circuit;
   - the flush-grace cutoff on the `pgx` hot form: P minus `FlushVisibilityGraceMs`, with a negative value read as `0`, never `sqlgen.FlushGraceCutoffDisabled` (design.md §6.1.1, *The widening is never disabled on this path*). The `attach` transport and the hot-excluded form keep today's resolution. Engine construction logs once when a negative value meets `pgx`, and the comments on `DuckDBConfig.FlushVisibilityGraceMs` and `WithFlushVisibilityGrace` say that the negative value applies to `attach` only;
   - classification: snapshot failures are `ErrPostgresReadFailed` and stay out of the breaker;
   - the disjointness guard;
   - `HotLegMaxDirtyRows`;
   - the execution plan's sources: the hot leg is recorded as a PostgreSQL source whose predicate PostgreSQL evaluates, in place of `hot-leg predicate (evaluated in DuckDB)`;
   - metrics: a latency stage for the snapshot reads, and a hot-match count beside `fed_query_row_count{source="pg"}`. Both change `docs/telemetry.md`.

   Validation: the federated e2e suite and the production harness run under both values of the switch. A mixed-tier tiebreak test walks one run of rows that tie on every column before `row_id`, some hot and some in parquet with their `row_id`s interleaved, one or two rows per page so that page boundaries fall inside the run and between a hot row and a parquet row. It walks the run under `created_at DESC, row_id DESC` and `created_at DESC, row_id ASC` cursors with no `AttributeOrders`, under an attribute sort with its required `row_id ASC` cursor, and as offset pages. Each walk must return every row exactly once, in the order DuckDB gives the same rows once all are flushed. A regression test covers a flush that races the query with the widening configured off. It reuses `runRacedQuery`'s pause after path resolution (`cdc_flush_grace_e2e_test.go`) with `FlushVisibilityGraceMs = -1` and the `pgx` transport. The racing flush appends and marks between P and the snapshot. Two variants: one creates four new rows, and the page must hold all eight rows, where `DisabledWideningExposesRace` sees four on `attach`; the other updates four flushed rows, and the page must hold their new values, not the versions in the resolved set. A unit test pins the cutoff: under `-1`, the `pgx` hot form's cutoff is the path-resolution instant, and the hot-excluded form and `attach` still get `sqlgen.FlushGraceCutoffDisabled`. `DisabledWideningExposesRace` stays pinned to `attach`. The other e2e tests that set `-1`, `TestFlushManifestWindowVisibility` and `TestFirstFlushAfterTheEmptyScanCheckStaysComplete`, assert complete answers and pass under both transports. Under `pgx` the widening keeps the second test's marked rows in the hot leg. Its slow-clock twin, whose marks fall below any cutoff, then carries the #720 recheck's coverage on that transport. A regression test covers publication after the snapshot over a non-empty glob. A schema read through the level-3 glob has parquet objects. A test hook between the snapshot and the DuckDB scan updates one clean row and flushes it, and deletes and flushes another. The page must hold the first row at its snapshot version and still hold the second.

   *Built (#856).* `DuckDBConfig.HotLegTransport` selects the pass, and `HotLegMaxDirtyRows` caps the dirty set it stages (unset by default). The factory gives the engine its pgx pool and the repository's hot-leg reads (`WithPgxHotLeg`) whatever the switch says, so changing the switch is the whole rollout and rollback. Decisions the steps above left open, and why:
   - *The parquet leg returns its sort keys.* Each row carries one column per `ORDER BY` term (`sqlgen.SortKeyColumn`), the expressions the statement orders by. The merge compares those with the hot keys. Re-deriving a parquet row's key from the returned record would reintroduce a type mapping (the record's, not the export's) that the parity work exists to avoid. For the same reason the order reads `visible`'s columns, qualified (`visible.<column>`), and never an output alias. A column-bound attribute orders by its parquet column at the export type, not by the outer select's image of its storage column: that image is a `SMALLINT` or a `VARCHAR` for a bound bool, which the comparator's `BOOLEAN` key refuses. And DuckDB binds an unqualified `ORDER BY` name to an output alias first, so an attribute named like an output column (`forma_sort_key_0`, say) would otherwise order by that alias instead.
   - *The snapshot stays open across the DuckDB statement,* as design.md §6.1.1 proposes, not the re-read-and-retry variant. A pass holds one pooled PostgreSQL connection for its whole duration.
   - *The breaker hears from DuckDB only.* The parquet leg records its success as soon as it has streamed. Every PostgreSQL failure of the pass (the snapshot, the dirty set, the hot keys, the page read) is `ErrPostgresReadFailed` and hands the probe back, and so does a refusal past `HotLegMaxDirtyRows` or a pass that runs no DuckDB statement.
   - *Totals.* The pass counts its matches exactly on every page: when the window carried no count (past the last parquet match, the #181 case), it recounts the parquet leg over the window `[0, 1)` on the same staged set and frozen objects, so `Query` no longer runs a second pass for it. The keyset coordinator's cursor-free recount is still a whole pass, which answers h plus the parquet count.
   - *#720.* A source-authored set whose globs list no object is decided in the snapshot. Without parquet history the hot leg is the answer and DuckDB runs nothing. With history, a source that now resolves objects raced a publication and the pass is retried once. One that still resolves nothing fails as a degradable `ErrFederatedReadFailed`. That is what the attach path's empty-glob scan ends in too, except that here no DuckDB statement runs, so the breaker does not count it.
   - *The disjointness guard* checks every parquet row against the whole staged dirty set, a superset of the hot keys, so it also catches a parquet row the anti-join should have dropped although no hot key names it. Its error, `ErrHotLegNotDisjoint`, is not degradable: the fallback would hide an internal inconsistency. Neither is a `pgx` transport on an engine built without `WithPgxHotLeg`.
   - *A tombstone newest in the window hides the row.* The step 4 hot-keys query served any `entity_main` row with an entry in the window. A row whose newest entry there is a tombstone, while `entity_main` still holds it, is a state only direct SQL plants (`TestInitSoftDeletedRowExcluded` does), and the attach path drops it: its dedup ranks the tombstone first, ties on `changed_at` included, and the visibility filter removes it. The query now drops it too.
   - *The execution plan* records the dirty set as read in the snapshot (the widened query in the hot form), the parquet-only statement, and the hot leg as a PostgreSQL source with `predicate_pushdown` set, whose row estimate is h and whose actual rows are the page's hot rows. `pg_snapshot` times the snapshot reads.

   Tests. Engine-seam tests run the pass over a real DuckDB and real parquet objects, with the snapshot in memory. They cover every offset and limit of the default order and of an attribute sort in both directions, keyset walks over runs that tie across the tiers (`created_at DESC` with `row_id DESC` and `ASC`, and `age ASC, row_id ASC`), offset pages and keyset walks over a `bool_smallint` and a `bool_text` bool and over an attribute named `forma_sort_key_0`, the hot-excluded form, the cap, snapshot failures and the breaker, the frozen inventory (a later object outside the scan, a listed object deleted before the scan or before its footer is validated, a failed listing), the three #720 outcomes, the plan and metrics, and no metrics when the row handler fails. A property test checks the merge's placement against a full sort of both legs at every offset. `TestPgxPassCutoffNeverDisablesTheWidening` is the cutoff unit test. In the production harness:
   - `TestPgxHotLegMixedTierTiebreakWalk` is the mixed-tier walk. ## Risks

- **Parity gaps the suites miss.** A predicate or ordering difference that no fixture reaches splits answers by tier silently: a row appears or disappears, or changes position, when it is flushed. The suites are the only guard, so fixtures come from the condition-DSL catalogue and from every derivation row, not from examples.
- **Hot rows that now fail the read.** A hot row whose stored image the OLTP read refuses (#708, #592) fails a federated page that answered before. The PostgreSQL route already fails on that row. Run the census before flipping the default, and repair what it reports.
- **Connection use.** A `pgx` pass holds one pooled PostgreSQL connection for its whole duration, DuckDB included. Size the pool for concurrent federated passes before flipping the default. If that is not affordable, use the re-read-and-retry variant from design.md §6.1.1.
- **A large backlog.** With CDC stalled, every pass reads and stages the whole dirty set. That is no worse than today, which copies all of `change_log`. `HotLegMaxDirtyRows` lets a deployment send such passes to PostgreSQL alone.

## Fallback

If the parity rulings or the tier parity suite stall, the alternative recorded in design.md §6.1.1 still removes `postgres_scanner`: stage the dirty rows in DuckDB over pgx and keep the merge there. Step 1, the staging half of step 3, and step 7 carry over. Steps 2, 4 and 6 are not needed for it.
